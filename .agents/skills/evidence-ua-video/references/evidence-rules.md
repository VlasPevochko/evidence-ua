# Evidence rules for Evidence UA video

These rules govern factual claims used in scripts, captions, charts, animations, thumbnails, and voiceover.

## 1. Claim ledger

Before scripting, create a compact ledger for every material claim:

| Field | Required content |
|---|---|
| Claim | Exact statement the audience will hear/see |
| Population | Who the evidence applies to |
| Exposure/intervention | What was tested |
| Comparator | What it was compared with |
| Outcome | What changed |
| Magnitude | Absolute/relative effect when available |
| Uncertainty | CI/CrI, heterogeneity, imprecision, or qualitative uncertainty |
| Dose/duration | If relevant |
| Source | Verifiable citation/URL/PMID/DOI |
| Status | supported / mixed / indirect / insufficient |

No numerical claim enters the script without a ledger row.

## 2. Evidence hierarchy

Prefer, when appropriate:
1. Current high-quality systematic reviews/meta-analyses.
2. Evidence-based clinical guidelines or consensus documents from authoritative bodies.
3. Well-designed randomized controlled trials.
4. Prospective observational studies.
5. Retrospective/cross-sectional studies.
6. Mechanistic, animal, and in-vitro evidence.

This is not a mechanical ranking: relevance, risk of bias, directness, precision, consistency, and recency matter.

## 3. Certainty language

Use wording proportional to evidence:

- Strong/direct: “показує”, “знижує/підвищує” only when causal evidence supports it.
- Moderate/mixed: “ймовірно”, “дані свідчать”, “може”.
- Observational: “пов’язано”, “асоціюється”.
- Mechanistic: “можливий механізм”, “теоретично може”.
- Insufficient: “наразі недостатньо даних”.

Do not label evidence “high/moderate/low GRADE” unless the source or your analysis actually used a formal certainty framework.

## 4. Numbers and charts

- Preserve units, denominators, follow-up duration, and population.
- Distinguish relative risk from absolute risk.
- Do not animate a point estimate without uncertainty when uncertainty materially affects interpretation.
- Do not truncate axes to exaggerate differences unless clearly justified and labeled.
- If a meta-analysis reports heterogeneity, do not imply every study produced the pooled result.
- Never convert a non-significant result into “no effect”; describe it as imprecise/compatible with the reported interval when appropriate.

## 5. Mechanisms

Mechanistic plausibility is not clinical proof.
Keep these visually and verbally distinct:
- molecular/cellular mechanism;
- physiological surrogate;
- performance/functional outcome;
- clinical outcome.

## 6. Population transfer

Explicitly flag extrapolation across:
- animals → humans;
- healthy → diseased;
- young → older adults;
- trained → untrained;
- acute → chronic;
- laboratory surrogate → real-world outcome.

## 7. Conflicting evidence

If credible sources disagree:
- state the disagreement;
- explain likely reasons (population, dose, design, bias, outcome definition, follow-up);
- do not cherry-pick the most dramatic result.

## 8. Health safety

Do not turn educational evidence synthesis into individualized diagnosis or treatment instructions. For clinically meaningful risks, contraindications, medication interactions, or red-flag symptoms, state the boundary and recommend appropriate professional evaluation when needed.

## 9. Source display

For short-form:
- show concise source identifiers on the relevant scene where feasible;
- include a final source card or caption list with enough information to verify each key claim.

For long-form:
- provide a full source list and map major claims to sources.

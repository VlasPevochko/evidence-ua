# Claim candidate dossier: exercise for rotator cuff-related shoulder pain (RCRSP)

Status: **research dossier, not a Claim.** Nothing here is approved or shown on the site. It follows `docs/EXERCISE_REVIEW_RULES.md`, section 3 (steps 1–2). Retrieval: PubMed, 2026-10-18. **Only abstracts were read**, not full texts. Every identifier below was returned by PubMed; it still needs a second check by a reviewer.

## 1. Bounded question

Population: adults with rotator cuff-related shoulder pain (synonyms in the reviews: rotator cuff tendinopathy, subacromial pain syndrome, partial-thickness tear).
Intervention: exercise programmes (strengthening, motor-control, scapula-focused).
Comparison: no treatment, placebo, non-specific exercise, or other exercise types.
Outcomes: shoulder pain, shoulder disability or function.

The question is about people **with** shoulder pain. It does not cover healthy people, prevention of injury, or post-operative rehabilitation.

## 2. Sources found (systematic reviews, from abstracts)

| # | Review | What the abstract reports | Certainty/limits stated |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Lafrance et al. 2024, JOSPT. [doi:10.2519/jospt.2024.12453](https://doi.org/10.2519/jospt.2024.12453) · PMID 38848304 | 22 RCTs, n=1281. Progressive and resisted exercise vs placebo/no treatment: uncertain clinical benefit. Non-progressive/non-resisted: no significant benefit. ## 3. What a bounded statement could look like (draft wording, unreviewed)

> In adults with rotator cuff-related shoulder pain, exercise programmes are commonly used. Reviews of randomised trials find that some programmes, such as motor-control and scapula-focused exercise, may slightly reduce disability compared with other exercise or conventional physical therapy, but the certainty is low to moderate, and it is unclear which component of the programme matters.

Wording rules from the review pack: no "treats", "cures" or "prevents". The statement says nothing about any specific exercise in the library.

## 4. Open issues before this can become a Claim

1. Full-text check of all five reviews (risk of bias, overlap of primary trials: reviews 1–4 likely share many trials, so they are **not independent confirmation**).
2. Decide the comparator (no treatment vs other exercise); the sources answer different comparisons.
3. Confidence assessment across the five domains by a reviewer; the abstracts alone do not support a rating.
4. Check for newer or Cochrane-level reviews and for retractions/corrections.
5. Clinician review of the final wording. Mapping to library entries (for example the rotator-cuff complex, scapular control) is a separate step: a review of a programme does not prove that a single video exercise has the same effect.
